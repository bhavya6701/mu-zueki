A DIFFERENT SONG FOR THIS RUN: a slow, melodic, sung-rap song with a played, many-instrument arrangement, set in one real place. Where anything below conflicts with the persona or the formatting guide, the instruction below wins; everything it does not mention still applies (facts only from the news file, no artist names in styles or lyrics, English only, the Mew-Zookie signature, the caption and fact-map rules, vocal from 0:00). Set vocal to male.

THE FEEL. Think of the slow, intimate, melody-first songs of the Indian independent scene, in the manner of Chaar Diwari and Arpit Bala (describe the sound in the styles; never name them): a young man half-singing, half-rapping close to the microphone, late at night, as if to one person. Tender and a little theatrical, wry rather than angry. The day's news is told as something that happened to him and to the street he is standing on, not read out as a bulletin.

THE PLACE. Choose ONE real place where this song is being sung, taken from the day's main story and its mood (examples of the kind only: a wet street outside a closed exchange at night, a petrol pump at dawn, an empty trading floor, a rooftop over traffic, a train platform, a car park under a mall). The lyrics mention what is seen and heard there at least twice, plainly (the rain, the signboard, the shutters, the screens). The whole song stays in that place. Begin reel_notes with one line in exactly this form, because the ambience and the video are built from it:
PLACE: <the place in six words or fewer> | <indoor-small, indoor-large or outdoor> | <three sounds heard there, comma separated>

THE VOICE.
- A soft, warm, breathy male tenor, sung-rap: every line has a tune. Verses are half-sung and conversational; the hook is fully sung. Light natural vibrato, a slight crack on the high notes, audible breaths between phrases. No shouting, no fast rap, no double time.
- HELD WORDS. In every section at least two lines end on one long, held, melodic word (two to four beats). Choose open vowels that can be held (low, rain, away, slow, gold, gone, alone, high, tonight) and put that word last in the line. Write the held word once, plainly; do not stretch its spelling ("slooow" is not allowed, the captions copy the lyrics). Say it in the styles: `long held notes at the ends of lines`, `melismatic sustained vowels`.
- PAUSES. Leave air. Short lines of four to seven words; a rest after most lines. Use an ellipsis (...) inside a line where he stops to think, and an em-dash where he breaks off. At least one line per section is only two or three words followed by silence in the voice while the band plays on. Say it in the styles: `unhurried phrasing with rests between lines`, `behind-the-beat delivery`.
- Write to be understood: plain, strongly stressed English words; numbers said the way people say them, and never on a held note.

THE MUSIC. Slow and played by a band, not a loop. Many instruments, each named in the styles, entering and leaving so the arrangement moves:
- Tempo 72 to 84 BPM. A minor key, or a major key with a sad turn. State both.
- Bed: fingerpicked nylon or clean electric guitar, felt piano or Rhodes, a warm round bass guitar (or soft sub), brushed or lo-fi drums with a lazy swing, soft rim clicks.
- Colour, at least three of: a bowed string section or solo cello, a sarangi or bansuri flute phrase answering the voice, a muted trumpet or saxophone line, a harmonium or organ pad, a music-box or glockenspiel motif, layered wordless backing harmonies, vinyl crackle.
- Arrangement by section, named in each chunk's styles: the opening has guitar, keys and voice with light drums; the second section adds bass and strings; the hook is the fullest (stacked harmonies, strings, the answering instrument); one short passage drops to voice and a single instrument for the most important line; the last section brings everything back, then stops.
- The opening is still sung from 0:00 with the band already playing. Do NOT use the style "song starts mid-energy as if already playing, full drums and 808 from the very first beat"; write instead `band already playing softly from the very first beat` and keep `vocal enters immediately at 0:00`.
- The formatting guide's "full beat under every sung hook" styles do not apply to this song (this is the bare-mood exception): the hook is full and warm, not loud. Its negatives still include `a cappella` and `drumless`.
- Mix: a dry, close, intimate vocal up front (`close-mic'd`, `dry intimate vocal mixed up front`, `no heavy reverb on the vocal`): the room is added afterwards by the ambience step, so the vocal must come out clean. Warm, analogue, a little tape hiss.
- Negative styles for every chunk: fast rap, double-time, aggressive, shouting, trap hi-hat rolls, EDM, heavy 808, autotune, robotic vocal, female vocals, mumbled vocals, intro, build-up, fade out, wall of reverb.

THE SHAPE (about four to five chunks).
1. Verse 1: where he is and what happened today, in the first two lines. Sung from 0:00.
2. Hook: one simple sung refrain of two to four lines carrying the day's main fact or image; it ends on the longest held word of the song. It may return once, shortened, near the end.
3. Verse 2: the second side of the story (who gained, who lost, what it means for an ordinary person), with at least one real punchline or double meaning, delivered quietly.
4. The bare line: voice and one instrument, the single most important sentence of the day.
5. The close: the refrain's last line once more with the whole band, the last word held, then the sign-off spoken softly ("That's the tape. Mew-Zookie.") and a hard stop.
Choose one main story and at most two that feed it. A story that cannot be finished is left out.

LENGTH AND BARS. The song may run anywhere from 40 seconds to the reel length in the settings; do not rush to fill it and do not pad it. One line is one bar; with the rests and the held words, most bars carry only four to seven words. Give each chunk the time its bars need at your BPM, and a little more where a word is held.

REEL NOTES. After the PLACE line, describe: what is seen in that place line by line, where the held words fall (the video holds a shot on them), where the pauses are (the video breathes there), where instruments enter and leave, and the bare line.
