A DIFFERENT SONG FOR THIS RUN: a mid-tempo, groove-led sung-rap song played by a full band with many instruments, set in one real place. It is a step faster and livelier than the slow night ballad of 2026-10-12 (78 BPM), but not a fast rap. Where anything below conflicts with the persona or the formatting guide, the instruction below wins; everything it does not mention still applies (facts only from the news file, no artist names in styles or lyrics, English only, the Mew-Zookie signature, the caption and fact-map rules, vocal from 0:00). Set vocal to male.

THE FEEL. A head-nodding, warm, slightly cheeky live-band hip hop groove: the kind of song a crowd sways to. Confident and wry, with a grin in the voice. The week's news is told as something happening around him in the place he is standing, not read out as a bulletin. It should be catchy enough that the hook sticks after one listen.

THE PLACE. Choose ONE real place where this song is being sung, taken from the week's main story and its mood (examples of the kind only: the steps outside a big bank's tower at closing time, a busy trading floor, a rooftop over traffic, a train platform at rush hour, a diner counter with a TV on, a street outside an exchange). The lyrics mention what is seen and heard there at least twice, plainly. The whole song stays in that place. Begin reel_notes with one line in exactly this form, because the ambience and the video are built from it:
PLACE: <the place in six words or fewer> | <indoor-small, indoor-large or outdoor> | <three sounds heard there, comma separated>

THE VOICE.
- A warm, clear male voice, half-sung half-rapped: the verses ride the groove in a relaxed, rhythmic flow with a tune in it; the hook is fully sung and catchy. Confident, playful, a smile in the voice. No shouting, no double time, no mumbling.
- A few held words: in the hook, the last word of at least two lines is held for two beats on an open vowel (high, low, gold, go, away, alright). Write the held word once, plainly. Say it in the styles: `held notes at the ends of hook lines`.
- Leave some air: a short rest after most lines; at least one line in the song is only two or three words followed by a band stab or a one-bar instrumental answer. Use an em-dash where he breaks off.
- Write to be understood: plain, strongly stressed English words; numbers said the way people say them, and never on a held note.

THE MUSIC. Faster and bouncier than 2026-10-12, played by a band, not a loop. Many instruments, each named in the styles, entering and leaving so the arrangement moves and the listener hears them change:
- Tempo 92 to 100 BPM. State the key (a minor key with a bright turn, or a major key).
- Groove: live drum kit with a tight snappy snare, crisp hi-hats with a light swing, a punchy kick; a funky round electric bass (finger-played, walking a little); hand claps or a tambourine on the backbeat.
- Harmony: Rhodes electric piano or clean jazzy electric guitar chords, a Hammond organ pad.
- Colour, at least four of: a brass section (trumpet, trombone, saxophone) playing short stabs and an answering riff, a funky wah or muted guitar lick, a bowed string section swell, a flute or bansuri riff, shakers and congas, a glockenspiel or vibraphone motif, layered backing vocal harmonies and call-and-response "hey" ad-libs, vinyl crackle.
- Arrangement by section, named in each chunk's styles: the opening has drums, bass, keys and voice already playing; the second section adds the brass stabs and claps; the hook is the fullest (brass riff, strings, backing harmonies, percussion); one short passage drops to voice, bass and drums for the most important line (a breakdown); the last section brings everything back with the brass riff, then stops.
- The opening is sung from 0:00 with the band already in the groove. Write `full band already grooving from the very first beat` and keep `vocal enters immediately at 0:00`. The formatting guide's "full beat under every sung hook" applies.
- Mix: a dry, close vocal up front (`close-mic'd`, `dry vocal mixed up front`, `no heavy reverb on the vocal`): the room is added afterwards by the ambience step, so the vocal must come out clean. Warm, punchy, analogue.
- Negative styles for every chunk: fast rap, double-time, aggressive, shouting, trap hi-hat rolls, EDM, heavy 808, autotune, robotic vocal, female vocals, mumbled vocals, intro, build-up, fade out, wall of reverb, slow ballad.

THE SHAPE (four to five chunks).
1. Verse 1: where he is and what happened this week, in the first two lines. Sung from 0:00.
2. Hook: one simple, catchy sung refrain of two to four lines carrying the week's main fact or image. It returns once, shortened, near the end.
3. Verse 2: the second side of the story (who gained, who lost, what it means for an ordinary person), with at least two real punchlines or double meanings, the funnier the better.
4. The breakdown: voice, bass and drums only, the single most important sentence of the week.
5. The close: the refrain once more with the whole band and the brass riff, then the sign-off ("That's the tape. Mew-Zookie.") and a hard stop.
Choose one main story and at most three that feed it. A story that cannot be finished is left out.

LENGTH AND BARS. The song may run anywhere from 40 seconds to the reel length in the settings; do not pad it. One line is one bar; at this tempo most bars carry six to nine words. Give each chunk the time its bars need at your BPM.

REEL NOTES. After the PLACE line, describe: what is seen in that place line by line, where the band stabs and instrument entries fall (the video cuts on them), where the breakdown is, and which lines carry a joke the video can play up.
